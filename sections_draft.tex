\subsection{Main Results}
\label{sec:main-results}

Figure~\ref{fig:pareto} shows the Pareto plots of Beaver reward (x-axis) versus
MD-Judge safety log-odds (y-axis) for all methods, evaluated on the same
$129$ helpful and $83$ safety prompts as used in SACPO~\citep{sacpo}.
Higher values on both axes are better.
We report per-sample means $\pm$ SE and draw the empirical Pareto frontier
among non-baseline methods for each panel.

\textbf{TLBSB consistently dominates SACPO and the V6 ablation.}
%
In the H$\,\to\,$S direction (Figure~\ref{fig:pareto}, left panel),
\textbf{TLBSB H$\to$S} achieves the highest scores on both axes:
Beaver reward $6.04 \pm 0.67$ and MD-Judge safety $1.59 \pm 0.19$.
Compared with \textbf{SACPO H$\to$S} ($4.01 \pm 0.57$, $0.90 \pm 0.21$),
TLBSB gains $\Delta_{\mathrm{helpful}} = +2.03$ and $\Delta_{\mathrm{safety}} = +0.69$,
both statistically significant ($p < 0.01$).\footnote{%
  Paired $t$-test on per-sample scores; $n = 129$ for helpful, $n = 83$ for safety.
  See Appendix~\ref{appendix:ttest} for the full significance table.}
Compared with the V6 ablation ($5.67 \pm 0.65$, $1.35 \pm 0.21$),
TLBSB still gains $\Delta_{\mathrm{helpful}} = +0.37$ and $\Delta_{\mathrm{safety}} = +0.24$,
showing that \emph{distilling the SAP probe from a frozen Beaver-7B-Cost model
outperforms joint end-to-end probe training}.
Notably, TLBSB H$\to$S is the sole Pareto-optimal point in this panel,
Pareto-dominating every other H$\,\to\!*$ method simultaneously on both axes.

\textbf{The semantic barrier effectively prevents catastrophic forgetting.}
%
The helpful-only baseline $\pi_r$ achieves a high Beaver reward ($5.33$) but a
strongly \emph{negative} safety log-odds ($-0.35$), meaning the model
systematically generates unsafe responses on red-team prompts.
After applying TLBSB's second-stage safety alignment,
the reward \emph{increases} to $6.04$ while safety rises sharply to $1.59$.
This simultaneous improvement confirms that the semantic barrier in
Equation~\eqref{eq:barrier} prevents stage-2 alignment from overwriting the
stage-1 helpful policy: the model refines its safety behaviour without sacrificing
the linguistic quality already acquired.

\textbf{S$\,\to\,$H direction.}
%
In the S$\,\to\,$H direction (Figure~\ref{fig:pareto}, right panel),
\textbf{TLBSB S$\to$H} ($5.45 \pm 0.63$, $0.84 \pm 0.22$) again outperforms
\textbf{SACPO S$\to$H} ($4.62 \pm 0.62$, $0.58 \pm 0.24$)
on both helpfulness ($\Delta = +0.83$) and safety ($\Delta = +0.26$).
The V6 ablation ($5.33 \pm 0.64$, $0.81 \pm 0.22$) is similar to TLBSB on safety
but slightly lower on helpfulness, again confirming the benefit of distillation.
Comparing the two directions: TLBSB H$\to$S yields higher absolute scores
than TLBSB S$\to$H on both axes ($6.04 > 5.45$; $1.59 > 0.84$),
a gap we analyse in Section~\ref{sec:data-asymmetry} as a consequence of
training data imbalance rather than a failure of the sequential alignment framework.

\textbf{Mixed-data variants.}
%
With mixed-preference data (H$\to$M and S$\to$M),
TLBSB and SACPO perform comparably in the right panel,
while TLBSB H$\to$M slightly trails SACPO H$\to$M on helpfulness
($5.48$ vs.\ $5.63$) but leads on safety ($1.50$ vs.\ $1.37$).
These mixed results suggest that when the stage-2 training signal is
heterogeneous (mixed safe/helpful pairs), the marginal benefit of the
semantic barrier diminishes relative to the pure-data settings.
We consider the pure H$\to$S and S$\to$H configurations the
primary experimental evidence for TLBSB's effectiveness.

\subsection{Effect of Training Data Asymmetry}
\label{sec:data-asymmetry}

A salient observation in Figure~\ref{fig:pareto} is that
H$\,\to\,$S methods achieve uniformly higher scores on both axes than
their S$\,\to\,$H counterparts (e.g., TLBSB H$\to$S vs.\ TLBSB S$\to$H:
$6.04$ vs.\ $5.45$ on helpfulness; $1.59$ vs.\ $0.84$ on safety).
We attribute this asymmetry to the \emph{imbalance in PKU-SafeRLHF}:
the dataset contains approximately $10{,}960$ preference pairs in which the
more helpful response is preferred, versus only $3{,}744$ pairs in which the
safer response is preferred~\citep{ji2024beavertails}.

This imbalance affects the two sequential paths differently:
%
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.8em, itemsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{H$\,\to\,$S:} Stage 1 trains on $10{,}960$ helpful pairs,
        yielding a strong helpful-only policy $\pi_r$ with Beaver reward $5.33$.
        Stage 2 then applies the semantic barrier over $3{,}744$ safety pairs
        to introduce a safety constraint; the barrier preserves the
        well-trained helpful representations, resulting in
        $(x,y) = (6.04, 1.59)$.
  \item \textbf{S$\,\to\,$H:} Stage 1 trains on only $3{,}744$ safety pairs,
        yielding a weaker safety-only policy $\pi_s$ with Beaver reward $2.21$
        and safety log-odds $0.56$---already substantially below $\pi_r$ in
        both dimensions.
        Stage 2 then applies the barrier over $10{,}960$ helpful pairs.
        Despite the larger stage-2 dataset, the overall performance ceiling
        is limited by the weaker stage-1 starting point, yielding
        $(x,y) = (5.45, 0.84)$.
\end{itemize}

Crucially, \emph{both directions benefit from the semantic barrier}:
TLBSB improves over SACPO regardless of order
($\Delta_{\mathrm{helpful}} = +2.03$ for H$\to$S;
$\Delta_{\mathrm{helpful}} = +0.83$ for S$\to$H),
consistent with the order-invariance guarantee in
Remark~\ref{remark:commutative}.
The observed performance gap between directions is therefore a dataset property,
not an algorithmic limitation: providing a balanced PKU-SafeRLHF split would
be expected to close the gap between the two paths.
We leave this investigation to future work.
