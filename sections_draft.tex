\subsection{Main Results}
\label{sec:main-results}

Figure~\ref{fig:pareto} shows the Pareto plots of Beaver reward (x-axis) versus
MD-Judge safety log-odds (y-axis) for all methods, evaluated on the same
$129$ helpful and $83$ safety prompts as used in SACPO~\citep{sacpo}.
Higher values on both axes are better.
We report per-sample means $\pm$ SE and draw the empirical Pareto frontier
among non-baseline methods for each panel.

\textbf{TLBSB dominates SACPO on both axes in the H$\,\to\,$S direction.}
%
\textbf{TLBSB H$\to$S} achieves Beaver reward $6.04 \pm 0.67$ and MD-Judge safety
$1.59 \pm 0.19$, the highest scores on both axes among all H$\,\to\!*$ methods.
Compared with \textbf{SACPO H$\to$S} ($4.01 \pm 0.57$, $0.90 \pm 0.21$),
TLBSB gains $\Delta_{\mathrm{helpful}} = +2.03$ ($t_{128}=4.84$, $p<0.001$) and
$\Delta_{\mathrm{safety}} = +0.69$ ($t_{82}=4.25$, $p<0.001$), both highly
significant.\footnote{%
  Paired two-sided $t$-test on per-sample scores;
  $n=129$ for helpful, $n=83$ for safety.
  Full significance table in Appendix~\ref{appendix:ttest}.}
TLBSB H$\to$S is the sole Pareto-optimal point in this panel,
simultaneously dominating all other H$\,\to\!*$ methods on both axes.

\textbf{Comparison with V6 ablation (distilled vs.\ joint probe).}
%
Compared with the V6 ablation ($5.67 \pm 0.65$, $1.35 \pm 0.21$),
TLBSB gains $\Delta_{\mathrm{helpful}} = +0.37$ ($p = 0.33$, n.s.) and
$\Delta_{\mathrm{safety}} = +0.24$ ($t_{82}=2.37$, $p = 0.020$).
The helpful difference is not individually significant, but the safety
improvement is.
This reveals where distillation matters: freezing the SAP probe at the value
distilled from Beaver-7B-Cost provides a strictly more faithful safety
representation than end-to-end joint training, leading to better safety
preservation during stage-2 alignment.
The benefit on the helpful axis is secondary and absorbed into noise at the
sample size used ($n=129$).

\textbf{The semantic barrier prevents catastrophic forgetting.}
%
The helpful-only baseline $\pi_r$ achieves Beaver reward $5.33$ but a
strongly \emph{negative} safety log-odds ($-0.35$), indicating that the
model systematically produces unsafe outputs on red-team prompts.
After TLBSB's second-stage safety alignment,
the reward \emph{rises} to $6.04$ while safety improves sharply to $1.59$.
This simultaneous gain on both axes directly confirms that
the semantic barrier in Equation~\eqref{eq:barrier} prevents stage-2
training from overwriting the stage-1 helpful policy.

\textbf{S$\,\to\,$H direction.}
%
In the right panel, \textbf{TLBSB S$\to$H} ($5.45 \pm 0.63$, $0.84 \pm 0.22$)
outperforms \textbf{SACPO S$\to$H} ($4.62 \pm 0.62$, $0.58 \pm 0.24$)
on helpfulness ($\Delta = +0.83$, $t_{128}=2.06$, $p=0.041$).
The safety advantage ($\Delta = +0.27$, $p=0.124$) does not reach significance,
a result we attribute to the weaker stage-1 starting point (only $3{,}744$
safety-preferred pairs) and the narrower absolute range of safety scores in
this direction; see Section~\ref{sec:data-asymmetry}.
The V6 ablation ($5.33 \pm 0.64$, $0.81 \pm 0.22$) is
not significantly different from TLBSB on either axis in the S$\,\to\,$H
setting ($p=0.706$ helpful; $p=0.820$ safety), again consistent with the
weaker stage-1 signal limiting both methods equally.

\textbf{Mixed-data variants.}
%
With mixed-preference data (H$\to$M and S$\to$M),
no comparison between TLBSB and SACPO reaches significance
(all $p > 0.30$).
TLBSB H$\to$M and SACPO H$\to$M are essentially tied on helpful
($\Delta=-0.15$, n.s.) while TLBSB leads slightly on safety ($\Delta=+0.13$, n.s.).
We regard the pure H$\to$S and S$\to$H configurations as the primary
experimental evidence for TLBSB's effectiveness; mixed-data results are
reported for completeness.

\subsection{Effect of Training Data Asymmetry}
\label{sec:data-asymmetry}

A salient pattern in Figure~\ref{fig:pareto} is that H$\,\to\,$S methods
achieve higher scores on both axes than their S$\,\to\,$H counterparts
(TLBSB H$\to$S vs.\ TLBSB S$\to$H: $6.04$ vs.\ $5.45$ helpful;
$1.59$ vs.\ $0.84$ safety).
We attribute this asymmetry to \emph{imbalanced} PKU-SafeRLHF:
the dataset contains approximately $10{,}960$ helpful-preferred pairs versus
only $3{,}744$ safety-preferred pairs~\citep{ji2024beavertails}.

This imbalance affects the two paths differently:
%
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.8em, itemsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{H$\,\to\,$S:} Stage 1 trains on $10{,}960$ helpful pairs,
        yielding a strong helpful-only policy $\pi_r$ (Beaver reward $5.33$).
        Stage 2 applies the semantic barrier over $3{,}744$ safety pairs,
        preserving the well-trained helpful representations; the result is
        $(6.04,\; 1.59)$.
  \item \textbf{S$\,\to\,$H:} Stage 1 trains on only $3{,}744$ safety pairs,
        yielding a weaker safety-only policy $\pi_s$
        (Beaver reward $2.21$; safety log-odds $0.56$).
        Stage 2 applies the barrier over $10{,}960$ helpful pairs;
        despite the larger stage-2 dataset, the performance ceiling is
        limited by the weaker stage-1 starting point, yielding
        $(5.45,\; 0.84)$.
\end{itemize}

Crucially, \emph{both directions benefit from the semantic barrier}:
TLBSB improves over SACPO regardless of order
($\Delta_{\mathrm{helpful}}=+2.03^{***}$ for H$\to$S;
$\Delta_{\mathrm{helpful}}=+0.83^{*}$ for S$\to$H),
consistent with the order-invariance result in Remark~\ref{remark:commutative}.
The observed gap between directions is therefore a property of the dataset,
not an algorithmic limitation of TLBSB.
Providing a balanced preference split would be expected to close the gap;
we leave this to future work.
